# Intel8080Chip — Half-Cycle-Accuracy Plan

## Goal

Convert `Intel8080Chip` from its current "one T-state per `Cycle()` call"
model to a half-cycle-accurate model driven by the chip's real clock input
pins, following the shape already established for `Mos6502Chip`, `TiaChip`,
and `Mos6532Chip`:

- `Intel8080Pins` (a separate mutable struct field, `Pins`) is deleted.
  Every pin becomes a property directly on `Intel8080Chip` — some
  plain auto-properties (inputs with no side effect), some backed by a
  private field with logic in the setter (inputs that drive behavior),
  some read-only (outputs).
- The chip is no longer advanced by an externally-called `Cycle()` method
  that does a whole T-state's worth of work in one call. Instead, the
  clock-input pin(s) are properties whose setters run only the work real
  hardware actually does at that specific edge — so a consuming system
  drives the CPU the same way it drives wires on a breadboard: by toggling
  clock pins, not by calling a "tick" method.

This is **not** a rewrite of instruction semantics. `HandleInstruction`'s
per-opcode `switch` and all the ALU helper methods stay as they are; only
*when* each pin read/write happens relative to the clock, and *how* pins are
exposed, changes.

## Why this is different from the 6502/RIOT/TIA conversions

Every chip converted so far has a **single external clock input**:

- `Mos6502Chip.Phi0` (real NMOS 6502: one input clock; the two-phase
  Φ1/Φ2 internal clocks are generated *inside* the chip and only exposed as
  read-only outputs — `Phi1 => !_phi0`, `Phi2 => _phi0`).
- `Mos6532Chip.Phi2` (real RIOT: single clock pin, no internal phase
  generator at all — timer ticks on the falling edge, bus access happens on
  the rising edge, gated on chip-select).
- `TiaChip.Osc` (single 3.58MHz master oscillator input, internally divided
  down to drive everything else, including the derived `Phi0` output to the
  6507).

In each case, "half-cycle-accurate" means: the *one* input clock pin's
setter does different, hardware-meaningful work on its rising edge vs. its
falling edge, instead of a single call doing a whole cycle's work at once.

**The 8080 does not have this shape.** Real 8080 hardware has no internal
clock generator at all — it takes **two independent external clock inputs,
Φ1 and Φ2**, both driven by an outboard clock-generator chip (the 8224),
with a specific non-overlapping relationship between them. There is no
single pin whose rising/falling edges bound a T-state; T-state timing is
defined by the Φ1/Φ2 relationship itself. So the accurate mapping here is
**two settable clock properties, `Phi1` and `Phi2`**, each of whose setters
carries only the piece of a T-state that real hardware does at that
specific edge — not a `Mos6502Chip`-style single `Phi0` property.

This is exactly the kind of detail the user asked to have grounded in real
reference material rather than inferred from the other chips already
converted, since inferring it from the 6502/RIOT/TIA precedent alone would
give the wrong pin shape.

## Clock-phase timing reference

Confirmed facts from the MCS-80/85 User's Manual (Ch. 4 prose + Ch. 6 AC
characteristics/pin-description tables), each tagged with confidence.
Everything here is **confirmed from the primary source** unless flagged
otherwise.

- **T-state boundary.** Φ1 and Φ2 are non-overlapping. A T-state is defined
  as the interval between two successive Φ1 rising edges — i.e. **every new
  T-state begins on a Φ1 rising edge**. Within a period, the order is Φ1↑,
  Φ1↓, (gap), Φ2↑, Φ2↓, (gap), next Φ1↑. Φ2's pulse is markedly wider than
  Φ1's (min 220ns vs. min 60ns at standard speed grade).
- **SYNC.** Rises on **Φ2↑ of T1** (marking the start of a machine cycle).
  Falls on **Φ2↑ of T2**.
- **External status latch (STSTB, off-chip, informs how a consumer like
  `SpaceInvadersSystem` should read the status word).** Generated by the
  8224 clock generator (not the 8228, correcting the assumption in the
  original research request), strobing low late in T1 — after status/SYNC
  are valid, before Φ2↑ of T2 clears them. A consumer reading `Data` while
  `Sync` is high (the current `SpaceInvadersSystem`/test pattern) already
  matches this correctly; nothing needs to change there.
- **Address bus.** Becomes valid on **Φ2↑ of T1**, stays valid until
  **Φ2↑ of the state immediately after T3** (i.e. through any inserted TW
  states).
- **Status word on the data bus.** Valid on the same **Φ2↑ of T1** edge as
  SYNC; cleared on **Φ2↑ of T2** (the same edge DBIN rises on) — for a
  write-type cycle, that edge is where outgoing write data substitutes for
  status instead. The CPU's own data-bus output buffer is switched off
  (high-Z) during T2/T3 of a *read*-type cycle, since the bus is driven by
  the addressed device instead.
- **DBIN.** Rises on **Φ2↑ of T2**; falls on **Φ2↑ of T3** — any inserted
  TW states between T2 and T3 extend it. Applies to Fetch, MemoryRead,
  StackRead, InputRead, and interrupt-acknowledge cycles.
- **Data sampling (reads).** The manual frames this as setup/hold windows
  (`tDS1` before Φ1↓ of T3, `tDS2` before Φ2↑ of T3, `tDH` after Φ2↑ of T3)
  rather than naming one latch instant, but since DBIN's trailing edge is
  Φ2↑ of T3 and the hold window is measured from that same edge, the
  effective internal latch point is **Φ2↑ of T3** — the same edge DBIN
  falls on.
- **WR.** Falls on **Φ1↑ of T3** (not a Φ2 edge — this is the one place the
  write side diverges from the read side's Φ2-referenced timing). Rises on
  **Φ1↑ of the state after T3**, extended by any inserted TW. Outgoing
  write data becomes valid on Φ2↑ of T2 (`tDD` delay) and stays valid
  through the rest of the machine cycle, so it's already stable well before
  WR's falling edge.
- **READY.** Sampled relative to **Φ2↓ of T2** (or of each TW, if already
  waiting) to decide whether the next Φ1↑ proceeds to T3 or inserts another
  TW.
- **HOLD.** Must be stable before a **Φ2↑** (the manual says "prior to...
  the rising edge of Φ2", without pinning down which specific Φ2 pulse
  beyond "during T2 or TW when entering hold mode"); the internal hold
  latch is set on a coincidence of READY, HOLD, and that Φ2 edge — i.e.
  HOLD can't be latched while a bus transaction is still stalled on READY.
- **HLDA.** ⚠️ **Unresolved conflict in the primary source itself**: Ch. 4's
  prose says HLDA is triggered by "the rising edge of the Φ1 clock pulse"
  (at the start of T3 for a read/input cycle in progress, or the state
  after T3 otherwise); Ch. 6's pin-description table says "the HLDA signal
  appears after the rising edge of Φ2" for the same event. The manual does
  not reconcile these. Since `Hold`/`HldA` aren't wired up by
  `SpaceInvadersSystem` today (see Open Questions), this plan defers
  picking one and documents the conflict at the point of implementation
  rather than guessing which chapter is right.
- **INT.** Setup relative to a Φ2 edge (`tIS`, min 120ns) during **the last
  T-state of the current instruction's last machine cycle**, without the
  manual further distinguishing Φ2↑ vs. Φ2↓ for this specific setup spec.
  INTE is a continuously-valid combinational gate (not independently
  clocked): "INTE F/F is reset if internal INT F/F is set" — i.e. accepting
  an interrupt and clearing INTE happen together, at **T1 of the
  interrupt-acknowledge fetch cycle**.
- **RESET.** ⚠️ **Not documented at clock-phase granularity.** The manual
  describes RESET only as level-sensitive ("while the RESET signal is
  activated...") with a minimum 3-clock-period pulse width — no Φ1/Φ2
  setup/hold spec is given, unlike READY/HOLD/INT which each get one. This
  plan treats RESET as level-sensitive without a specific sampling edge,
  since that's genuinely all the primary source documents.
- **HALT (TWH).** Entered after T2 of the machine cycle following the HLT
  opcode's fetch. Exits to T1 on **Φ1↑** for both the interrupt path and
  the "HOLD line goes low" path; RESET exits unconditionally to T1
  (level-sensitive, as above).

## Derived per-T-state edge protocol

Mechanically walking the timing reference above against the *existing*
`Cycle()` switch shows that today's per-`(machineCycleType, state)` pin
assignments already fall on a small, consistent set of real edges — this
is what makes the split into `Phi1`/`Phi2` setters a largely mechanical
transformation rather than a re-derivation of every opcode:

| Today's dispatch key | Pin action | Real edge |
|---|---|---|
| `*T1` (`Address = ...`) | Address bus loaded | Φ2↑ |
| `*T1` (`Data = StatusWordX`, `Sync = true`) | Status word + SYNC valid | Φ2↑ (same edge as Address) |
| `*T1` (`Wr = true`) | WR deasserted (left over from a possible previous write) | Φ1↑ (start of T1 itself) |
| `*T2` (`Sync = false`) | SYNC cleared | Φ2↑ |
| `*T2` (`DBIn = true`, read-type cycles) | DBIN asserted | Φ2↑ (same edge as SYNC clearing) |
| `*T2` (`Data = _tmp`, write-type cycles) | Outgoing write data valid | Φ2↑ (same edge) |
| `*T2`→T3 boundary, READY check | Wait-state decision | Φ2↓ of T2 (or of each TW) — decision only, no pin change; a "not ready" result repeats T2-shaped behavior instead of advancing to T3 |
| `*T3` (`DBIn = false`, read-type cycles) | DBIN deasserted, data latched | Φ2↑ |
| `*T3` (`Wr = false`, write-type cycles) | WR asserted | Φ1↑ (start of T3 itself — the one case that's Φ1-referenced, not Φ2) |
| `*T4` (`Wr = true`, extended write cycles e.g. XTHL's 5-state StackWrite) | WR deasserted | Φ1↑ (start of the state after T3) |
| `FetchT1` (`IntE = false`, when accepting an interrupt) | INTE cleared | Φ1↑ (start of T1 — "at time T1 of M1" per the manual, which doesn't further specify Φ1 vs. Φ2 within T1; Φ1↑ chosen as the literal start of the state) |

Everything in `HandleInstruction`'s opcode-specific switch that has **no**
externally-visible pin manifestation (register moves, ALU ops, `_wz`
bookkeeping — e.g. `FetchT4`/`FetchT5` internal work, or the
"`FetchT2`: executes during the fetch of the next instruction" ALU calls)
has no datasheet-specified timing of its own. The rule this plan uses:
internal-only work rides on whichever edge its current `*Tn` label maps to
above (Φ1↑ for T4/T5-only states with no generic pin action of their own),
which exactly preserves today's observable T-state-level ordering and
behavior — the thing that's actually pin-visible and testable — while
making the chip's internal state machine genuinely Φ1/Φ2-edge-driven
rather than driven by one call that lumps a whole T-state together.

## Current shape (for contrast)

`Intel8080Chip.Cycle()` is called once per T-state by the embedding system
(`SpaceInvadersSystem.TickCpu`, gated on a `_masterClock % 10 == 0`
divider). Each call:

1. Runs a big `switch (machineCycleTypeAndState)` that sets up
   `Pins.Address`/`Pins.Data`/`Pins.Sync`/`Pins.Wr`/`Pins.DBIn` for whichever
   T-state it's in — all pin transitions for the *entire* T-state happen in
   this one call, with no distinction between what would be an "edge 1"
   effect (e.g. address becoming valid, SYNC rising) vs. an "edge 2" effect
   (e.g. SYNC falling, DBIN rising) on real hardware.
2. Advances `_state` (T1→T2→T3→T4→T5).
3. Calls `HandleInstruction`, which reads/writes pins and internal registers
   according to `_ir` and the current `(machineCycleType, state)`, and calls
   `SetNextCycle` to move to the next machine cycle when a T-state's real
   hardware work is done.

The consuming system then inspects `Pins.Sync`/`Pins.DBIn`/`Pins.Wr`
*after* `Cycle()` returns to service the bus (supply read data, capture
write data) — i.e., the whole read/write handshake for a T-state is
resolved synchronously around one `Cycle()` call, rather than the address
being stable for one part of the period and data being latched at a
specific later edge, the way real hardware (and a real system's ROM/RAM
decode logic wired to the actual pins) would see it.

## Target shape

- `Phi1` and `Phi2` become the two clock-input properties, each with a
  private backing field and edge-gated logic in the setter (mirroring
  `Mos6532Chip.Phi2` / `TiaChip.Osc`'s `if (_x == value) return;` guard
  style, but — unlike those single-clock chips — each of `Phi1`/`Phi2` only
  does real work on *one* of its two transitions per the table above
  (`Phi1` on rising, `Phi2` on both rising and falling); the other
  transition is a no-op guard, same shape as today's single-clock chips
  use for their inactive edge).
- All other pins (`Address`, `Data`, `Sync`, `Wr`, `DBIn`, `Reset`, `Hold`,
  `Int`, `IntE`, `Wait`, `Ready`, `HldA`) become properties directly on
  `Intel8080Chip`, replacing every `Pins.X` read/write in
  `Intel8080Chip.cs` with plain `X`. Phase 1 leaves every one of these as a
  plain public-get/public-set auto-property (see Phase 1 below); Phase 2 is
  where each one's *true* accessibility gets assigned, mirroring the
  three shapes `Mos6502Chip.cs` already establishes for exactly this kind
  of pin:
  - **CPU-driven outputs** — pins only ever assigned inside
    `Intel8080Chip.cs` itself, never by a consuming system — become
    `{ get; private set; }` auto-properties: get-only from outside the
    class, settable (via the property itself, not a separately-named
    field) from anywhere inside it. This is `Mos6532Chip.Irq`'s shape
    (`Mos6532Chip.cs:65`), not `Mos6502Chip.Address => _address`'s
    manual-backing-field one — the manual-field shape only earns its
    keep when the getter computes something (e.g. `Mos6502Chip.Phi1 =>
    !_phi0`); a pin that's pure storage with no setter logic has nothing
    for a named field to buy over the compiler-generated one, and reusing
    a single name (`Address`) for both the internal write and the
    external read beats juggling `_address`/`Address` as two names for
    the same value. Applies to `Address`, `Sync`, `DBIn`, `Wr`, `IntE`,
    `HldA`, `Wait` — all seven are exclusively chip-driven in the current
    `Cycle()` body and in `SpaceInvadersSystem`/`Intel8080ChipTests`,
    which only ever *read* them.
  - **Genuinely bidirectional bus** — `Data` keeps a public getter *and*
    setter, the same as `Mos6502Chip.Data` (`Mos6502Chip.cs:19-24`, with
    its own `// TODO: Make this tri-state` note carried over here too),
    since both the chip (write cycles, status word) and the consuming
    system (supplying read data) legitimately assign it depending on the
    current machine cycle.
  - **External inputs** — pins only ever assigned by a consuming system,
    never by `Intel8080Chip.cs` itself — get an `internal get`/public
    `set`, the same write-mostly shape as `Mos6502Chip.Nmi`/`.Irq`/`.Rdy`
    (`Mos6502Chip.cs:232-256`: "exposed for testing, even though this is
    a write-only pin"). Applies to `Reset`, `Hold`, `Int`, `Ready` — none
    of these are ever assigned by the chip itself, only sampled (or, for
    `Reset`/`Hold`/`Ready`, not yet sampled at all — see Open Questions).
    Keeping the getter `internal` (rather than dropping it) matches the
    precedent of leaving it available to `Intel8080ChipTests.cs` and
    similar internal callers even though it's a write-only pin from a
    real consuming system's perspective.
- `_machineCycleType`/`_state`/`_machineCycle` and the whole
  `HandleInstruction` opcode `switch` stay conceptually the same in shape
  and content — same `(machineCycleType, state)` dispatch keys, same
  opcode cases — but the work currently lumped into one `Cycle()` call
  gets distributed across the `Phi1`/`Phi2` setters per the edge protocol
  table above.
- `SetNextCycle`'s `_state = State.T1` advance moves to fire on `Phi1`'s
  rising edge (the real T-state boundary), replacing the current
  unconditional per-`Cycle()`-call `_state` increment.
- `Intel8080Pins.cs` is deleted.

## Implementation phases

**Phase 1 — mechanical: `Pins` struct → properties, `Cycle()` unchanged.**
Exactly mirrors how `TiaChip`/`Mos6532Chip` were converted first (commits
`1bf6605`, `b3b273d`): every `Intel8080Pins` field becomes a property
directly on `Intel8080Chip` (plain auto-properties for now), every
`Pins.X` reference across `Intel8080Chip.cs`, `Intel8080Debugger.cs`,
`SpaceInvadersSystem.cs`, `SpaceInvadersDebugger.cs`, and
`Intel8080ChipTests.cs` becomes `X`, `Intel8080Pins.cs` is deleted, and
`Cycle()` keeps working exactly as it does today (called once per
T-state). This is a pure rename/reshape with no behavior change, so the
existing `Intel8080ChipTests` cycle-count assertions should pass unmodified
— useful as a checkpoint before the riskier Phase 2 rewrite.

**Phase 2 — split `Cycle()` into `Phi1`/`Phi2` edge dispatch.**
Using the derived edge-protocol table above:
- Assign every pin its true accessibility per the three shapes in Target
  shape above: `Address`/`Sync`/`DBIn`/`Wr`/`IntE`/`HldA`/`Wait` become
  `{ get; private set; }` (get-only from outside `Intel8080Chip.cs`,
  settable from anywhere inside it); `Data` keeps public get/set; `Reset`/`Hold`/`Int`/
  `Ready` become `internal get`/public `set`. `Phi1`/`Phi2` get their own
  private-backing-field/edge-gated-setter treatment as described below,
  rather than either of the other three shapes. This is a mechanical
  accessibility change with no behavioral effect on its own — do it first,
  before the edge-dispatch rewrite, so a build break here can't be
  confused with an edge-dispatch bug.
- Replace `Cycle()` with two setters, `Phi1` and `Phi2`.
- `Phi1`'s rising-edge handler: advances `_state`/`_machineCycle`/machine
  cycle type (today's `SetNextCycle` logic, now edge-triggered instead of
  called unconditionally), then dispatches every pin action and opcode
  case currently keyed to a `*T1` (the `Wr = true` reset and any
  `IntE = false` interrupt-acceptance case) or a `*T3`/`*T4` write-cycle
  `Wr` transition, plus all internal-only T4/T5 opcode work.
- `Phi2`'s rising-edge handler: dispatches every pin action currently keyed
  to `*T1`'s `Address`/`Data`/`Sync` assignment, `*T2`'s `Sync`/`DBIn`/
  write-`Data` assignment, and `*T3`'s `DBIn = false` + data-latch opcode
  cases.
- `Phi2`'s falling-edge handler: the READY-sampling wait-state decision
  (today's `// TODO: Sample READY and HOLD pins` at `FetchT2` — see Open
  Questions on scope).
- The giant `switch (_ir) { ... }` in `HandleInstruction` keeps its
  existing per-opcode structure and `(machineCycleType, state)` case
  labels; only *which setter* ends up calling into it, per case, changes.
  No opcode's semantics should need to change — this phase is a dispatch
  reorganization, not a re-implementation.
- Update `Intel8080ChipTests.cs` and `SpaceInvadersSystem.TickCpu` to drive
  `Phi1 = true; Phi1 = false; Phi2 = true; Phi2 = false;` (in that order,
  once per T-state) instead of calling `Cycle()`, and to increment
  `cycleCount`/service the bus once per T-state rather than once per
  setter call — see the `Intel8080ChipTests.cs` row in Systems / files
  affected below for why the expected cycle counts themselves must not
  change.

**Phase 3 (stretch/optional) — real Φ1/Φ2 waveform timing in
`SpaceInvadersSystem`.** Phase 2 makes the *chip* genuinely edge-driven
internally, but `SpaceInvadersSystem`'s master-clock loop can keep calling
the four setters back-to-back within its existing divided tick without
reproducing the real non-overlapping Φ1/Φ2 duty cycle (Φ2 pulse width is
~3.6× Φ1's per the AC characteristics table). Actually spacing the four
edges out over sub-divisions of the current `_masterClock % 10 == 0` window
— ideally checked against Space Invaders' actual arcade clock-generator
schematic rather than invented — and moving bus servicing from
"after all four edges" to the specific edge each transition documents
(e.g. supply read data right as `DBIn` rises rather than after the whole
T-state) would be a further-fidelity pass, not required for Phase 1/2's
goal of exposing pins as properties and making the chip edge-driven.

**Phase 3 implementation notes.** Space Invaders' own clock-generator
schematic (a custom counter chain off the 19.968MHz crystal, since this
board doesn't use an 8224) wasn't locatable despite searching
computerarcheology.com's hardware writeup, MAME's `mw8080bw`/`8080bw`
driver source, arcade-museum.com repair/schematic threads, and the
`clash-spaceinvaders` FPGA reimplementation — none document the generator's
own Φ1/Φ2 duty cycle at the gate level. Rather than invent one, the four
edges are spread across the existing 10-master-tick T-state window using
the *MCS-80/85 User's Manual*'s own AC-characteristics ratio (min 60ns Φ1
vs. min 220ns Φ2 at standard 2MHz speed grade — already cited above) scaled
to this window's ~50ns/tick granularity: Φ1 high for 2 ticks (~100ns), a
1-tick gap, Φ2 high for 5 ticks (~250ns), a 2-tick gap before the next Φ1
rising edge (`SpaceInvadersSystem.TickCpuClock`). Bus servicing (`ReadCpuBus`/
`WriteCpuBus`, split out of the old combined `TickCpu`) now runs at the
specific edge each transition documents — read data supplied on `DBIn`'s
rising edge, writes committed on `WR`'s falling edge — rather than once
after all four edges of a T-state.

## Open questions to resolve before/during implementation

- **`Ready`/`Hold`/`HldA` are currently unused** — `SpaceInvadersSystem`
  never drives `Ready` or `Hold`, and the existing `Cycle()` body has a
  `// TODO: Sample READY and HOLD pins` at `FetchT2` that was never filled
  in. The timing reference above documents where these *should* be sampled
  (READY at Φ2↓ of T2/TW; HOLD near a Φ2↑ of T2/TW), but real HLDA timing
  has an unresolved conflict in the manual itself (Φ1↑ per Ch. 4's prose
  vs. Φ2↑ per Ch. 6's pin table). Since Space Invaders' hardware doesn't
  appear to need WAIT-state insertion or HOLD/HLDA at all, this plan
  recommends leaving them a documented TODO (as today) rather than picking
  an unverifiable edge for HLDA — revisit only if a future system actually
  needs them.
- **RESET's clock-phase timing isn't documented** by the primary source at
  all (level-sensitive only, no Φ-edge setup/hold spec given, unlike
  READY/HOLD/INT). The existing `Res`/`Reset` handling (not yet present in
  `Intel8080Chip` — there's no reset logic in the current `Cycle()` at all,
  actually; worth confirming during implementation whether reset was ever
  wired up) can stay level-sensitive without picking a specific edge, since
  that's genuinely all the datasheet supports.
- **`SpaceInvadersSystem`'s master-clock divider** (`_masterClock % 10 ==
  0`) currently stands in for "one 8080 T-state per 10 master-clock ticks"
  at a single call-site. Phase 2 can keep this exact divider and just fire
  all four `Phi1`/`Phi2` edges at that single tick (no waveform fidelity
  change, only internal chip restructuring). Phase 3's real duty-cycle
